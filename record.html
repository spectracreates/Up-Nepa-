Four numbers, four different arguments

Most electricity arguments are two people using different metrics. Pick one and see what it measures, and what kind of statement it supports.

Figure 5 — The neighbours

Availability, not capacity, is the difference

South Africa was in a worse electricity crisis than Nigeria in 2023. It recovered mainly by getting existing plants running again, not by building new ones. That makes plant availability the fairest number to compare.

The two regulators define their factors slightly differently, so treat this as close rather than exact. South Africa passed 476 consecutive days without load shedding in September 2026. Egypt runs a surplus and exports to Jordan. Sources: NERC 2026 Q1 report; Eskom system statements, 2026.

Unresolved

The sources disagree on grid collapses

This page does not pick one. Both counts are published and they do not reconcile, which matters because collapse frequency is one of the few measures where the picture looks like clear improvement.

  • One account: the grid went down almost monthly in 2024, with two major collapses in 2025.
  • Another: ten collapses in 2024 and four in 2025.
  • Both agree 2025 was better than 2024. Further collapses were reported on 29 December 2025, 23 January 2026 and in early February 2026.
Limits

What this page cannot tell you

01

National megawatts are not your light. Distribution, load-shedding and band classification sit between the grid and your socket. A good national quarter can coincide with a terrible month on your street. That is what the live tracker is for.

02

Averages hide the shape. An average of 4,100 MW is consistent with steady mediocre supply and with wild swings between blackout and surplus.

03

Reclassification moves people between bands. A household can pay more with no change in supply, or get the same hours under a different label.

04

Reported figures are revised. Quarterly reports restate earlier numbers. Figures here are as published at the date compiled.

05

Off-grid supply is invisible here. More than 60% of manufacturers took captive generation permits between 2024 and 2025. None of that appears in grid figures.

Method

Where every number came from

All figures come from published regulatory reports or from reporting that cites them. Nothing here is modelled, estimated or interpolated. Where two sources conflict, both are shown. Where a quarter is missing, it is left out rather than filled in.
